Environmental screening for mineral exploration in Nevada

Most exploration targeting starts with geology. Our team started with the question of where exploration would face the fewest environmental constraints, and built a statewide model to answer it.

My role
GIS Team Lead
Study area
State of Nevada
Analysis units
11,341 hex cells, 10 mi² each
Presented at
Society of Economic Geologists conference

The approach

We divided Nevada into a tessellation of 11,341 ten-square-mile hexagons and scored every cell on three families of environmental constraint. Each input layer was reclassified to a common 1–5 risk scale so they could be combined, then merged in a weighted, null-safe composite. Natural Breaks (Jenks) split the composite scores into four zones: minimal, low, moderate, and high risk.

1. Surface water

Stream and waterbody features from the USGS National Hydrography Dataset, filtered by flow permanence. To refine drainage in target areas, USGS 3DEP LiDAR was processed into a terrain model in GRASS GIS and derived flow paths (Fill, Flow Direction, Flow Accumulation, stream extraction).

2. Groundwater

Basin-level risk from the Nevada Division of Water Resources 2023 Groundwater Basin Indicators: water level decline, water rights commitments, surface water capture, and proximity to mine dewatering, weighted 30/25/25/20.

3. Biodiversity

A composite of 41 threatened and endangered species, habitat, and migration corridor layers. Species weren't treated equally. I scripted a tiered weighting based on federal ESA status (endangered over threatened over sensitive) and how restricted each species' range is, so a narrow-range endangered species counts for more than a widespread sensitive one.

Automating it with ArcPy

Joining dozens of species layers to 11,341 cells by hand wasn't realistic. I wrote ArcPy scripts in an ArcGIS Pro notebook to batch-clip inputs, run the bulk species joins, calculate the 1–5 reclassification, apply the species weights, and compute the final composite score.

Locating prospects

Alongside the environmental model, the team narrowed down prospect areas using target geology, mapped faults, and regional geophysics. We also used hydrothermal alteration mapping, classifying AVIRIS-5 hyperspectral imagery with Spectral Angle Mapper, using a custom endmember library built from the USGS spectral library and resampled with NumPy to the sensor's band centers.
